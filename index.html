Brian McCauley, PhD

I am a marketing researcher exploring how esports and gaming communities are built, managed and legitimised, from grassroots LAN events in Sweden to the organisations and cities that develop around them.

My work examines how people play, consume and form identities online, how gaming brings people together offline, and how value is created where digital and physical worlds meet (aka the phygital). As a qualitative researcher, I study gaming's role in digital society through the lenses of marketing, sustainability and entrepreneurship. I also develop auto-netnographic methods that use diaries of play to explore lived gaming experiences.

About

I am an Assistant Professor of Marketing at the Media, Management and Transformation Centre (MMTC), Jönköping International Business School, Jönköping University. My research looks at gaming culture, grassroots esports, phygital community management, esports legitimisation and employer branding through esports.

I work from Jönköping, the former "City of DreamHack", and much of my research happens with the people who build esports locally: LAN organisers, the Swedish Esports Federation, Esport United, DreamHack, Glitched and Jönköping Municipality. I also advise Jönköping Municipality on its regional strategy for esports and gaming.

As founding Vice Chair of the Esports Research Network, I have helped build the field's academic infrastructure since 2019: the inaugural ERN Symposium, the first in-person dedicated esports research conference (ERNC22), and editorial work on the journals that publish the field.

Publications

  • 2026
    Managing phygital experiences in grassroots esports: human-centered orchestration in the Swedish LAN-scape. McCauley, B., Berndt, A., & Helmefalk, M. Journal of Services Marketing.Key insights
    Key insightsInterviews with 28 LAN organisers
    • LAN parties blend a physical venue with digital play, and Swedish organisers manage that blend mainly through people rather than technology.
    • The paper introduces human-centred phygital orchestration: how organisers, volunteers and community norms coordinate the physical and digital sides of the experience.
    • It extends Batat's phygital customer experience framework and Baudet and Parmentier's four phygital strategies into grassroots esports.

  • 2026
    The future of esports sustainability: interdisciplinary pathways and challenges in an AI-mediated digital transformation of society. Scholz, T., & McCauley, B. Journal of Electronic Gaming and Esports.Key insights
    Key insightsConceptual paper
    • Revisits the sustainability debate in esports and maps the interdisciplinary pathways needed to move it forward.
    • Considers how AI-mediated digital transformation changes the challenges facing players, organisations and governance.
    • Treats gaming and esports as conceptually distinct but practically inseparable when thinking about sustainability.
  • Forthcoming
    Disruption by esports: the digital transformation of media businesses. Guest editors' introduction. McCauley, B., & Scholz, T. Journal of Media Business Studies.Key insights
    Key insightsSpecial issue introduction · six papers
    • Frames esports as a disruptor of media business across three domains: digital media environments, stakeholder interactions and institutional interfaces.
    • Each domain disrupts something different: business models, producer and audience logics, and organisational structures.
    • Positions phygital orchestration as a layer that cuts across all three, and sets out four directions for future research.

    Exploring esports as a platform to develop organizational teams: the case of Valorant. McCauley, B. Team Performance Management.Key insights
    Key insightsAuto-netnography · Valorant
    • Draws on an auto-netnographic study of team play in the shooter Valorant to explore how esports can support team development inside organisations.
    • Shows how communication, defined roles and coordination under pressure in play map onto workplace teamwork.
    • Points to esports as a practical tool for team building and, by extension, employer branding.

  • 2022
    Current issues of sustainability in esports. Nyström, A., McCauley, B., Macey, J., Scholz, T., et al. International Journal of Esports. Most citedKey insights
    Key insightsWorkshops, ERN Symposium 2019
    • Social sustainability is the main concern: player burnout and injury, limited diversity, and toxicity in online spaces.
    • The industry structure is incomplete, with inconsistent standards, contested governance, publishers holding most of the power, and integrity risks.
    • The business logic is immature: revenue models are weak, careers are short and training is underdeveloped.

  • 2020
    'Smashing it': how user entrepreneurs drive innovation in esports communities. Koch, N., Pongratz, S., McCauley, B., & Achtenhagen, L. International Journal of Esports, 1(1).Key insights
    Key insightsCase: European Super Smash Bros. scene · 12 interviews
    • User entrepreneurs are driven by belonging and care for the community rather than money, and often pay for events themselves.
    • Their insider knowledge lets them see needs that outside organisers miss.
    • Limited publisher support created uncertainty, but also an improvising, welcoming culture that helped the scene innovate.
